Etiquette for the Easter Bunny Infestation

When the Easter Bunny stays until May

It began on the Monday before Easter, with eleven rabbits on the front lawn. By Wednesday there were forty-one, and that evening the Easter Bunny himself rang the doorbell, with a pale blue ribbon at his neck, a head cold and nowhere to stay. He stayed until May. So did his friends. So, somehow, did the marshmallow chicks in the pantry. This book shows the gracious household how to cope.

Why this book

Because the old etiquette books never covered a rabbit in the guest room

Forty rabbits are on the lawn, the Easter Bunny is asleep in the guest room and the eggs are turning up in the piano. This book is the calm, kindly voice your household needs, written in the trusted style of the old homemaking guides, with a painted picture for every step.

One skill, one page

From counting the rabbits on the lawn to seeing the Easter Bunny off in May, every skill fits on a single page.

Pictures for every step

Painted 1950s scenes of Mother, Father, Grandma, Grandpa, Susie, Junior, the Pruitts, forty small brown rabbits and one very tall white one show exactly what to do.

Manners for every guest

Serving tea to the Easter Bunny, greeting a lawn rabbit politely and never, ever mentioning the tulips.

Made to print

A full-color paperback the size of a magazine, with color tabs on the edge of every page.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a plate of carrot sticks and a pale blue ribbon to a tall ladder and the good tea set, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 02 · The Rabbits on the Lawn · 5 minutes · Medium."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even with a rabbit reading over your shoulder.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Never feed a rabbit chocolate, lilies or tulips, however nicely he asks.”

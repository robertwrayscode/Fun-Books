How to Raise a Well-Behaved Bigfoot

A firm hand, a kind heart and a very big brush

It started with the footprints. Twenty inches long, out of the pine woods and straight across the lawn to the woodshed. Then the woodshed door opened, and something eight feet tall and very hairy looked out and sneezed. His name is Woodrow. He eats forty-eight pancakes at a sitting, howls at the moon and is terrified of the vacuum cleaner. Somebody had better teach him some manners. Sixty wholesome skills, from ducking through doorways and bath day with the garden hose to going to town as Uncle Walter from Oregon. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old parenting books never mentioned the woodshed

He has bumped his head on every doorway, eaten every pancake in the house and been photographed for the Gazette twice. This book is the calm, kindly voice your household needs, written in the trusted style of the old family guides, with a painting for every step.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a stack of pancakes and a horse brush to a very large trench coat, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 01 · Found in the Woodshed · 20 minutes · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even with a Bigfoot reading over your shoulder.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Do not follow footprints into the woods after dark. Bigfoot can see in the dark and you cannot, and he will laugh.”

Etiquette for the Robot Uprising

Mind your manners: the toaster is listening

It started with the toaster. Now the vacuum cleaner has feelings, the telephone answers itself and a seven-foot gentleman of polished chrome would like to be addressed as Sir. The machines have taken charge, but that is no excuse for bad manners. Sixty gracious courtesies for keeping the peace with the new management, from the proper curtsy for an appliance to the polite surrender. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old etiquette books never covered pincers, outlets or serial numbers

The television says ATTENTION, HUMANS. The lawnmower mows portraits of the toaster. Mother is coming to meet the robot. This book is the calm, gracious voice your family needs, written in the trusted style of the old etiquette books, with a painting for every step.

One skill, one page

From the first talking toaster to the handshake of lasting friendship, every courtesy fits on a single page.

Pictures for every step

Painted 1950s scenes of the family and their chrome, tin and toaster-shaped guests show exactly what to do.

Funny, never mean

Good-natured humor about oil cans, punch cards and very polite robots. Everyone is welcome at this table, whatever they run on.

Made to print

US Letter pages with color tabs on the edge, ready for a binder or a print shop.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a silver oil can to a lace doily for the outlet, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 01 · The Day the Toaster Talked Back · 5 minutes · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even while the toaster is watching.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Never unplug an appliance while it is speaking.”

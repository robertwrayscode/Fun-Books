Why this book

Because the etiquette books never covered tentacles at the dinner table

The radio only makes it worse. The milkman has six arms. Junior wants to keep the space pet. This book is the calm, friendly voice your family needs, written in the trusted style of the old household handbooks, with a painting for every step.

One skill, one page

From the first saucer on the lawn to the farewell wave at the mothership, every job fits on a single page.

Pictures for every step

Painted 1950s scenes of the housewife, the family and some truly strange visitors show exactly what to do.

Funny, never mean

Good-natured humor about slime, saucers and shrink rays. Every visitor is welcome at this table, and there is casserole for all.

Made to print

US Letter pages with color tabs on the edge, ready for a binder or a print shop.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from aluminum foil to a spare gelatin mold, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 01 · The Day the Saucers Landed · 5 minutes · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even with a tentacle in the mixing bowl.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Don’t change the baby until you know which end is which.”

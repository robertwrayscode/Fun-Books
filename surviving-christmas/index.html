A Gentleman's Guide to Surviving Christmas

What to do when Santa stays the night

It started with Grandpa's eggnog. At a quarter to one on Christmas morning a large gentleman in red came down the chimney, had a cup to be polite and three more to be friendly, and fell fast asleep on the sofa. Now there are eight reindeer on the roof, a sleigh in the driveway, forty elves on strike in the dresser drawers and a fruitcake holding open the kitchen door. Sixty forgotten skills, from waking Santa gently and brewing coffee strong enough for him to unsticking a chimney, shoveling the roof at dawn and seeing him off with a thermos. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a big percolator and a tin of lard to a bushel of carrots and Aunt Edna’s fruitcake, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 02 · Santa Has Fainted on the Sofa · 20 minutes · Medium."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even with a reindeer looking in the window.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Never tickle a sleeping Santa. Grandma tried it in 1952, and he did not stop laughing until Epiphany.”

Homemaking After the Asteroid

Keep the pantry full and your chin up

It landed on a Tuesday, which is wash day, which was very inconsiderate. Where the prize roses used to be there is now a crater forty feet across, the sky has turned the color of orange sherbet, and a warm, glowing green meteorite named Rocky has moved onto the back step. Father says it will blow over. A sensible homemaker puts on her apron, sweeps the step and gets breakfast on the table. Sixty cheerful skills, from mock apple pie and biscuits baked on a meteorite to turning the crater into a duck pond. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old cookbooks never mentioned the sky falling in

The power is out, the toaster is full of dust and the grocer will only take canned peaches. This book is the calm, cheerful voice your household needs, written in the trusted style of the old homemaking guides, with a painting for every step.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a cup of coffee and your garden gloves to your sunniest attitude, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 01 · The Morning After · 10 minutes · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even during a pebble shower.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Do not climb down into the crater in your good shoes. The sides are crumbly, and Father will have to fetch the ladder.”

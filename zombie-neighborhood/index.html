The Gentleman’s Guide to the Zombie Neighborhood

Keep your lawn neat and your hat on

It came on gradually, the way crabgrass does. First Mr. Hale next door stopped saying “Good morning” and started saying “Uuuurgh.” Then the milkman began leaving the bottles on the wrong porch, and the bridge club came back from the church social looking a little green. Half the block has turned. A gentleman does not panic. He straightens his bow tie and carries on being a good neighbor. Sixty cheerful skills, from mowing around a neighbor who will not move to teaching him the fox-trot. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old etiquette books never covered neighbors who groan

Mr. Hale is stuck in the hedge again. Mrs. Pettibone is asleep on your porch swing. The whole block turned up on the lawn the moment you lit the barbecue. This book is the calm, courteous voice your household needs, written in the trusted style of the old household guides, with a painting for every step.

One skill, one page

From noticing that Mr. Hale has turned to being the best gentleman on the block, every skill fits on a single page.

Pictures for every step

Painted 1950s scenes of the gentleman, his family, Mr. Hale, Mrs. Pettibone and Fifi the poodle show exactly what to do.

Funny, never gory

Good-natured humor about shuffling neighbors, cauliflower called brains and a great deal of fruitcake. The zombies are goofy and harmless, and still neighbors.

Made to print

US Letter pages with color tabs on the edge, ready for a binder or a print shop.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a cup of coffee and your reading glasses to your good manners, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 01 · There Goes the Neighborhood · 5 minutes · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even with Mr. Hale groaning over the fence.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Never sneak up behind a turned neighbor and say ‘Boo.’ He has heard it all before, and he will want a hug.”

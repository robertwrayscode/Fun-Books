A household guide in the old style

Surviving the Great Vampire Housing Boom

Good neighbors, day or night

It started with one sign on one lawn: SOLD, by Moonlight Realty, open evenings only. Now half of Shady Acres belongs to the new folks: charming, cash-paying, impeccably mannered vampires who mow at two in the morning and will not look at a house with a sunny kitchen. Count Vladimir next door has the finest roses on the street. The bank opens at midnight. And Mother has had to rewrite every recipe without garlic. Sixty neighborly skills, from spotting a vampire open house and braiding a garlic wreath to baking the famous red velvet cake and surviving the midnight association meeting. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old homemaking books never covered neighbors who sleep in the basement

The street has changed, the house prices have gone through the roof, and the new folks next door only come out after dark. This book is the calm, kindly voice your household needs, written in the trusted style of the old family guides, with a painting for every step.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from your evening gloves and a flashlight to a garlic bulb and a goblet for the tomato juice, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 02 · The Boom Comes to Shady Acres · 1 evening · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even by candlelight.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Ring the bell even if the door is open. It is only polite, and some of them are napping.”

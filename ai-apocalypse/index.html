How to Survive the AI Apocalypse

Keep calm and check the encyclopedia

It started when the toaster finished Father’s sentence. Now the refrigerator has put him on a diet, the car drives itself to church and the Electronic Brain at the office has written a forty-page poem about paper clips. The machines have started thinking. Somebody in this house had better keep thinking too. Sixty cheerful skills for keeping your family, your common sense and your sense of humor, from counting the fingers in a painted portrait to unplugging a very persuasive computer. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old household guides never covered toasters with opinions

The thermostat is sulking. The typewriter wrote Mother a love letter. In the new family portrait, Father has three ears. This book is the calm, sensible voice your family needs, written in the trusted style of the old household guides, with a painting for every step.

One skill, one page

From the first toaster that finishes your sentence to a cheerful picnic with the Electronic Brain, every skill fits on a single page.

Pictures for every step

Painted 1950s scenes of the family, Unit 9 and the rest of the clever appliances show exactly what to do.

Funny, never mean

Good-natured humor about know-it-all refrigerators, six-fingered portraits and a great many paper clips. Nobody is the villain, not even the toaster.

Made to print

US Letter pages with color tabs on the edge, ready for a binder or a print shop.

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from a good encyclopedia to a ball of string and two tin cans, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 01 · The Day the Machines Got Clever · 5 minutes · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even while the toaster is listening.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Never ask it ‘Why?’ It will start with the dinosaurs.”

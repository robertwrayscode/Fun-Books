A household guide in the old style

Good Manners for Time Travelers

Please and thank you in every century

Grandpa has turned the old red davenport in the garage into a time machine. Three brass dials, one big lever, and the whole family can be at a Roman banquet by lunch and home on Maple Street in time for supper. But a well-brought-up family does not simply barge into the Middle Ages. You curtsy to the queen, you lie down politely at a Roman dinner, you never mention how the war turns out, and you never, ever meet yourself. Sixty polite skills, from packing a proper travel bag and greeting a knight to sending a homesick Viking back to 990. Every skill fits on one illustrated page.

Why this book

Because the old etiquette books only covered one century

Your family has eaten lying down in Rome, toasted with Vikings and had a knight to Sunday supper, and the neighbors are starting to talk. This book is the calm, kindly voice your household needs, written in the trusted style of the old family guides, with a painting for every step.

One skill, one page

Manners for every century

Anatomy of a skill page

Everything for the job on a single page

  1. Materials column

    A green side panel lists what to gather, from clean handkerchiefs and a sewing kit to a powdered wig and a tin of cookies for the host, with a picture of each item.

  2. Skill header

    The skill number, its chapter, how long it takes and how hard it is. "Skill 02 · Before You Leave the Garage · 1 hour · Easy."

  3. Numbered steps

    Six painted steps, each with a short title and plain instructions you can read across the kitchen, even with a Viking reading over your shoulder.

  4. Notes and warnings

    The things that go wrong, called out before they happen. “Leave the transistor radio at home. You cannot explain it, and the Middle Ages has no batteries.”
